# Optimality of CGLMP Measurements for Maximally Entangled Qutrits

**Gurvir Singh** · Harish-Chandra Research Institute

[Read the paper](paper/cglmp_d3.pdf) · [LaTeX source](paper/cglmp_d3.tex) · [Lean proof](lean/CGLMP3_GlobalOptimality.lean) · [Verification instructions](lean/README.md)

This paper gives a geometric perspective on CGLMP measurement optimality: the four relative measurement bases form a unitary loop with cyclic-shift holonomy. For qutrits, commutators in $SU(3)$ encode the Bell cost, and the standard Fourier-phase measurements realize a balanced four-step factorization.

## Result and scope

**The optimality result established here is for $d=3$.** For the fixed maximally entangled state

$$
|\Phi_3\rangle=\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle+|22\rangle}{\sqrt3},
$$

and arbitrary complete rank-one local projective measurements, two per party,

$$
I_3\leq\frac43+\frac{8}{3\sqrt3}=2.872934051\ldots.
$$

The standard CGLMP measurements attain equality. The competing measurement bases are arbitrary complex unitary matrices; no Fourier or symmetry ansatz is imposed.

The theorem above fixes the maximally entangled state and concerns complete rank-one projective measurements; it does not cover optimization over states or arbitrary POVMs.

## Geometric insight beyond qutrits

Although **this work proves global optimality only for $d=3$**, its geometric framework extends to higher dimensions. The paper develops the unitary-loop formulation, harmonic commutators and simultaneous angular-budget inequalities, and equal sharing along the Fourier path. These structures explain how the standard measurement family fits the geometry and isolate the remaining obstruction: a sharp joint cost inequality coupling harmonics generated by the same unitary. The higher-dimensional analysis provides insight and a concrete target for extending the geometric proof; it does not prove global optimality for $d>3$.

## Relation to the PSI result

David Turturean and Ning Bao's [*The optimal CGLMP measurements on maximally entangled states*](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/oqp-batch-2026/blob/7049357c42a8fe85262da08fdc10d57b4d9e67a0/oqp27-cglmp-inequalities/paper/oqp27-cglmp-inequalities.pdf), released by Physical Superintelligence (PSI), establishes projective-measurement optimality in arbitrary outcome dimension by a different analytic approach.

Our qutrit optimum, $4/3+8/(3\sqrt3)$, agrees with the $d=3$ Fourier-phase value in that result. The Fourier-path expression studied in our higher-dimensional discussion is the same standard Fourier-phase candidate whose global optimality their work establishes. Our contribution emphasizes the geometric explanation through holonomy, commutators, and balanced angular displacement; our own global proof and Lean-verified optimum are confined to $d=3$.

## Lean verification

The `lean/` folder is a standalone Lean 4 project pinned to **Lean 4.24.0 / Mathlib v4.24.0**. The theorem `CGLMP3SOS.full_global_optimality` proves the universal bound and existence of attaining measurements.

The formal proof uses an **exact tracial sum-of-squares certificate**. It verifies the same qutrit optimal value by an algebraic route; it does not formalize every argument in the paper, including Thompson's theorem, the angular-budget argument, higher-dimensional statements, or the paper's rigidity conclusions.

The delivered source compiled locally with exit code 0. Its final theorem depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. The certificate is checked with exact arithmetic in Lean, without a numerical solver or proof placeholders. See the [verification record](lean/verification.log).

```sh
cd lean
lake update
lake exe cache get
lake env lean CGLMP3_GlobalOptimality.lean
```

Initial setup downloads the pinned dependencies; exact certificate checking may take several minutes.
